About the Founder

Six years of go-to-market work. One habit that hasn't changed.

Every time the ceiling got close, I went and learned the next skill myself — from content, to CRM systems, to enterprise GTM, to building an AI product. This is that story.

2020

It started with a dare.

In the first weeks of the COVID lockdown, I was a psychology student writing overly philosophical Instagram captions. A friend read one and told me, half-joking, to stop philosophizing and go get a job.

I took the dare seriously — nobody knew when college would reopen. I started applying to content marketing roles with no portfolio, just conviction, and had an interview call within three days. The final question from the person who'd become my boss: name three bloggers. I said Komal Pandey and That Boho Girl. I got the job.

Six years later, that's still the story I tell when people ask how this started.

2020 – 2022

Learning by doing.

Over the next two years, I led content and campaign strategy for restaurant launches across two cities, then moved into influencer marketing for a skincare startup — negotiating with creators, managing deliverables, learning the unglamorous mechanics behind every polished campaign.

When COVID-19 hit its second wave, I helped run a public-health content collaboration between two hospital systems and a major payments platform. It was the first project I worked on with real stakes — and the first time I understood how fast good marketing has to move when it actually matters.

2022 – 2023

Systems thinking.

Marketing had stopped being personal for me and started being something I wanted a career in, so I went back for an MBA in Marketing & Sales. Restless from lockdown, I also took an evening role at a startup called Gyanwave — and that's where marketing automation clicked for me: running one campaign across multiple channels from a single platform.

For my MBA research, I built a complete CRM strategy from scratch for a healthcare client — mapping the entire decision system, from IVR to CRM to sales outreach — and trained their sales team to run it. Their sales grew 5x.

2023

The one I wanted.

Placement season is where I learned what I actually wanted. I'd already cracked most of the interviews I sat for, but none of them were the one I was holding out for. I remember sitting in a room, close to giving up, when a friend pulled me out and told me flatly: you're getting Moody's — you're not selling credit cards.

He was right. That offer is still one of the best days of my career — three years at a global company that took me from lending SaaS launches to a board meeting in Paris.

2023 – 2026

Owning the GTM engine.

At Moody's Banking Unit, I owned go-to-market strategy for the Lending Solutions portfolio — positioning, campaigns, and content for twelve global product launches, working with stakeholders across the US, UK, and Costa Rica.

I used LLMs to run competitor and customer research that fed directly into ICPs, sales plays, and battlecards. I built a performance dashboard in a week that was estimated to take six. And I watched a single SEO-led landing page influence one of the largest deals of the year.

2026

There's always more to see.

Three years in, I understood my own potential better than when I'd started — and knew it stretched further than the role I was in. I left Moody's in September 2026 to go all-in on AI and automation, because I could see where the industry was heading and wanted to be building in it, not just reacting to it.

Marketing got me here. AI is where I'm taking it next.

"My dream is to contribute to people's dreams, so they can grow and feel proud of what they build, just as I am living mine."

I'm building the same kind of systems now — research, content, and reporting that used to take a team weeks — except this time, I'm the one building them, not just running them. If there's a thread through all of it, it's this: I don't wait to be handed the next skill. I go get it.
